Social systems

Was the first protest the cause, or the last grain of sand?

Why did one act of protest in Tunisia in December 2010 spread within weeks across much of the Arab world? An earlier study read it as a butterfly effect, a small initial event amplified by chaos. Our book chapter argues that this reading leaves out the state those societies were already in.

We turn to self-organized criticality and its sandpile model. Grains added one at a time bring a pile to a state where the next grain may shift a few neighbors or release an avalanche. In our reading, decades of economic hardship, political repression and inequality in Tunisia, Egypt and neighboring countries were the accumulating grains, and the protest in Tunisia was the last grain on a pile that was already critical.

The chapter is a qualitative interpretation and does not fit a model to protest data. It suggests that suppressing such pressures delays a transition and makes it more violent, and it leaves open where the transition ends.

Publication

Ş. N. Açıkalın, E. C. Artun The Concept of Self-Organized Criticality: The Case Study of the Arab Uprising Chaos, Complexity and Leadership 2017 · Springer, 2019
