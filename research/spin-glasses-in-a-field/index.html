Magnetism and phase transitions

What does the direction of a magnetic field do to a spin glass?

A magnetic field favors spins that point along it. In a model where spins can point only along the coordinate axes, a diagonal field can favor several directions equally. How does that choice affect a magnet whose bonds already disagree?

We compared fields along an axis, a face diagonal and a body diagonal. Across the systems studied we found 17 distinct phases. Diagonal fields left room for glassy order in two- and three-component models because aligned and alternating spin patterns could still compete. In some cases the glassy phase continued from zero field; in others it appeared only once a field was applied. The calculations are exact on the chosen hierarchical lattice and give an approximation for an ordinary cubic lattice.

Publication

E. C. Artun, D. Sarman, A. N. Berker Axial, Planar-Diagonal, Body-Diagonal Fields on the Cubic-Spin Spin Glass in d=3: A Plethora of Ordered Phases under Finite Fields Physical Review E, 2024arXiv
