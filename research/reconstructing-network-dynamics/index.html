Signals and networks

How much of a network’s wiring survives in its signals?

A network can be easier to observe than to map. We might record the combined activity of several nodes while knowing little about which pairs interact. How much of the wiring survives in that signal?

In an ongoing collaboration, we test this question on simulated networks whose connections we know. A recurrent neural network retains a short history of the observations. We train a linear readout of its activity to estimate the connections, then compare its estimates with the original network. Hubs and communities are our initial test cases as we add noise or combine measurements. Earlier work by Irem Topal and Deniz Eroglu used sparse recovery to infer the dynamics of coupled chaotic units.
