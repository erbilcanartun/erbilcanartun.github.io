Approach

I work between fundamental research and its applications.

In research I study how collective behavior emerges in systems of many interacting parts. Some of this work runs forward, from the rules of a model to the phases, transitions and chaos they produce, with the tools of statistical physics. Some runs backward, from recorded data to the system behind it, as in reconstructing a network from its signals.

In industry I work on decision intelligence: building a mathematical or AI-based model of an operation, simulating how it behaves under different scenarios, and using the results to compare decisions before they are made. I have also set up and managed research programs. Both kinds of work rest on a model of how the system works, one that can be tested against data and used to explore what has not happened yet.

About

Physics, complexity and philosophy

I am a physicist and philosopher based in Istanbul. I hold a PhD in Computational Sciences and Engineering from Kadir Has University, an MA in philosophy, and BS and MS degrees in physics from Yeditepe University.

My doctoral research with A. Nihat Berker used renormalization-group theory to study phase transitions, spin-glass chaos and emergent order. I now study geomagnetic signals as a postdoctoral researcher at TÜBİTAK’s National Metrology Institute and work as a data scientist at MetLife Türkiye. Before that, at TRK Technology, I developed market indicators and founded a quantum computing program. I have also taught graduate and undergraduate physics.

Philosophy has run alongside the physics. My MA thesis was on Spinoza’s Ethics. I have spoken on interpretations of quantum mechanics, on self-organized criticality in the Arab uprisings, and on the work of art in an age of non-material production, and I have shared critical essays through the Interval blog. Ethical and political theory remain close interests.
