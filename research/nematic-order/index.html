Magnetism and phase transitions

Can a disordered magnet order like a liquid crystal?

A compass needle distinguishes north from south. A collection of spins can agree on a north–south axis even if half point each way. This kind of alignment is called nematic order and is familiar from liquid crystals.

We found it in a three-dimensional model magnet with randomly competing bonds. With three or more spin components, warming the frozen glass first produced nematic order. At higher temperature even the common axis disappeared. The strength of the chaos inside the glass, measured by its Lyapunov exponent, alternated between odd and even numbers of components.

Publication

E. C. Artun, D. Sarman, A. N. Berker Nematic Phase of the n-Component Cubic-Spin Spin Glass in d=3: Liquid-Crystal Phase in a Dirty Magnet Physica A, 2024arXiv
