Magnetism and phase transitions

When can a single line of spins order?

A chain of spins changes character when distant pairs can still feel each other. By tuning how quickly their interaction fades with distance, we can ask when an entire line of spins will order at a temperature above absolute zero.

Our Ising-model preprint found both conventional magnetic order and frozen glassy order for suitable long-range interactions, but no alternating order: with every pair of spins interacting, alternation cannot satisfy them all. A follow-up preprint calculates magnetization curves and critical exponents across the whole range. The exponents change continuously as the interaction is made to fade faster or slower. In the inverse-square case the magnetization jumps at the transition while the specific heat shows an essential singularity, a hybrid of first- and second-order behavior that rigorous results had predicted.

In a published study of the spin-1 Blume–Capel chain, each site also had a nonmagnetic state. At some parameter values, cooling first produced a magnetized phase and then restored disorder as nonmagnetic sites became favorable. That return to disorder is an example of reentrance.
