Electronic transport

How often does an electrical current jump?

A current that looks smooth over a long interval can make occasional large jumps between readings. In a conference poster, I revisited measurements from two glassy semiconductor films to see how often jumps of different sizes occurred.

I measured the change between consecutive readings and fitted the distribution for each film with a q-Gaussian curve. Both had heavier tails than a normal distribution, with fitted q values of 2.5 for silver-containing arsenic sulfide and 2.7 for aluminum-containing arsenic selenide. The tails capture the comparatively frequent large changes in these data.
