Magnetism and phase transitions

How large can a pocket of the old phase survive?

After a magnetic field changes, a small patch of spins can keep its earlier orientation even when the field favors a different one. The size of the patch helps determine whether that old arrangement can persist.

In three-dimensional Potts models, we held the boundary of a finite region in its original state and calculated how its interior responded. This gave a limiting droplet size for each temperature, field and number of spin states. Sweeping the field in opposite directions also traced different magnetization curves. In some cases the two branches of this hysteresis loop had visibly different shapes.
