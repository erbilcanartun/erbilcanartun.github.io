Doctoral thesis

How complexity emerges across scales

How do simple rules between neighboring spins produce phase transitions, new kinds of order and even chaos? Why do materials that differ in their microscopic details share the same behavior near a transition? My doctoral thesis approaches these questions by following one system through a sequence of enlarging blocks. At each step, the question is which details can be set aside and which still govern what the material does.

I used real-space renormalization, a calculation that replaces the interactions inside a block with effective interactions between blocks. On hierarchical lattices this step can be carried out exactly. The way these effective interactions change from one scale to the next tells which phase a system is in. In spin glasses they wander chaotically, and the strength of that chaos stayed the same from the smallest spin to the infinite-spin limit. Other chapters find a liquid-crystal phase in a disordered magnet, seventeen phases under magnetic fields, a fractal surface that freezes while the interior stays disordered, and finite-temperature order in a one-dimensional chain with long-range interactions.
