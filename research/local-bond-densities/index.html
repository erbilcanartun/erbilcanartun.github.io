Magnetism and phase transitions

Can disagreement between neighbors grow as a magnet cools?

As a magnet cools, neighboring spins usually agree more often. Can a particular kind of boundary between unlike neighbors actually become more common on the way to an ordered state?

We counted the probabilities of neighboring spin pairs in three-dimensional Potts and clock models. Tracking these local probabilities as spins were grouped into blocks exposed a detail that the overall magnetization hides. In a state with a preferred orientation, the density of certain unlike pairs rose over part of the cooling range before falling to zero at zero temperature.
